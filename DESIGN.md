# Ambient dashboard

Reference: Paper Ambient Landscape supplied by the owner. A wall display in a softly lit home should feel quiet, readable, and stable.

Use system sans, normal weights, and a light clock face. Evergreen background oklch(24.30% .0194 167.17), raised surface oklch(29.53% .0268 163.93), sage accent oklch(82.38% .0619 130.22), primary text oklch(92.82% .0140 124.56), muted text oklch(74.91% .0230 149.61), outline oklch(39.45% .0284 156.49). The raised surface is for controls and floating elements (hovered and selected buttons, the settings popover, the toast) and for the alternating fill of the event cards inside Up next and daily forecast cards inside Weather; a widget's own background never uses it. These inner cards share a 1px outline and 12px radius. All widgets have a 1px outline and 18px radius. No decorative shadows except floating controls.

Clock digits use tabular numbers with light tracking (-0.025em), keeping minutes distinct while retaining a compact clock face.

Every widget uses `WidgetFrame`, which owns outer padding, responsive padding rules, the header-to-content gap, and the remaining content area. Individual widgets must not duplicate or override that frame spacing. The frame renders the shared `WidgetHeader`: a muted, normal-weight heading at the top left, with an optional `actions` snippet aligned to the right. Use normal reading and keyboard order for controls; insert a new control before existing ones to grow the row from right to left while keeping the rightmost control anchored. Titles truncate before controls are squeezed. The actions row is 20px tall like the heading; buttons retain their 44px minimum touch target, extending into the surrounding spacing without pushing the heading down. Headers stay at the top in loading, empty, and not-connected states; shared messages start directly below the header using the normal header-to-content gap.

To add a widget, supply a title, content through the default children snippet, and optionally header controls through the named actions snippet:

```svelte
<WidgetFrame title="Example">
  {#snippet actions()}
    <button onclick={refresh}>Refresh</button>
    <button onclick={openMenu}>Options</button>
  {/snippet}

  <p>Widget content</p>
</WidgetFrame>
```

Omit the actions snippet when there are no controls. Content is centered by default; a widget can give its own content wrapper `flex: 1` to fill the available space, as the event list does. Widget-specific spacing such as card padding and forecast gaps stays inside that content. The frame also exposes `--widget-content-height` for content sizing, so the clock does not repeat header or outer-padding calculations. `WidgetHeader` remains usable independently where a full widget frame is not needed.

`WidgetFrame` also accepts an optional `message` prop with `{ title, detail }`. A message replaces the content snippet with shared, top-aligned text directly below the header; the content snippet is not rendered while the message is present. This applies equally to empty, unavailable, and not-connected messages. Header actions remain available, so each widget decides whether a control still makes sense in that state. Omit `message` or pass `null` to show normal content. Message titles show at most two lines, or one in widgets at most 120px tall.

Widgets decide what message to pass; the frame does not inspect data or source status. Use `message={notice ?? emptyMessage}` so a source notice takes precedence over a valid empty result. Source notices still come from `sourceNotice`, while the widget supplies its own empty-result text, such as "No upcoming events" or "No upcoming bills". Empty, unavailable, and not-connected states share presentation, not meaning.

The clock shows a "Clock" heading above the time and the weekday and date.

The month calendar is its own widget, with Sunday-first weeks and today highlighted. When the widget is at least 280px tall it shows the whole month with room for a six-week month. Shorter widgets show only the current week. The month is read-only and updates with the local date; it is distinct from the Up next widget, which shows the next event.

Up next lists events under an "Up next" header, each as a card: the widget's 1px outline with a 12px radius, and every other card filled with the raised surface colour, so both themes follow their own palette. All-day events lead their day. The first card's time uses the accent colour. There is no fixed event-count cap or count-specific size breakpoint: the list measures its available height, card heights, and gaps to show as many whole cards as fit. A ResizeObserver remeasures when the list or cards resize, and the measurement updates when the event data changes. Overflow cards remain hidden and excluded from the accessibility tree without shrinking visible cards or showing partial ones. If not even one card fits, none is shown; a lack of space is not treated as an empty calendar. Empty and source-status messages start directly below the header, like all shared widget messages.

Bills uses the same card list as Up next under a "Bills" header: each bill is a card with its name and due date on the left and its amount on the right, with every other card filled with the raised surface colour. Cards run soonest due first, the first card's due date uses the accent colour, and only whole cards that fit are shown. Because bills are typed into a file, its empty and error states use their own wording: "No bills yet" when there is no file, "Bills unavailable / Check your bills file" when it is invalid, and "All clear" when it lists none.

The display is landscape only and uses one fixed layout on a 24 by 24 grid: Clock and Weather across the top, then Month calendar, Up next, and Bills below. Landscape uses 48px outer padding and 24px gaps. All widgets have a 1px outline and a background that matches the page. Widgets never change position, emphasis, or size at runtime, and nothing takes over the screen.

Settings has one entry point: a gear beside the location in the header. The header carries no tabs or Home button, because the dashboard is home nearly all the time and permanent navigation on an ambient display only invites stray touches. The gear opens Settings as a full screen over the dashboard, which keeps updating beneath it. A left rail lists the sections (Bills, Appearance), the content scrolls on its own, and a Done button or Escape closes it and returns focus to the gear. With no touch for 90 seconds it closes by itself, so it is never left open on the wall. Settings has an address, `#/settings/bills`, so it can be opened directly. It is built for touch: targets of at least 44px, a stepper for the due day instead of typing, a switch for autopay, and a second tap to confirm a delete. On a narrow screen the rail becomes a row across the top. The bills editor lists bills as rows that echo the Bills cards, and editing replaces the list with a form so the small LCD never shows both. Evergreen remains the default; Turquoise uses deep teal surfaces and pale turquoise accents with the same spacing and contrast hierarchy. The browser stores the selected theme. A shared toast component dismisses after 4.5 seconds, pauses on hover/focus, and handles repeated notices.

Weather centers current conditions and forecast in the content area below its header, with a shared left inset. The large icon beside the current temperature shows the current conditions, using the same icon set as the forecast cards, and stays optically aligned to forecast text. After dark a clear or partly cloudy sky is drawn with a moon, using Open-Meteo's day-or-night flag; forecast cards always stay daytime pictures, since a day is not a moment. The daily forecast shows today plus four more days in a single row. Daily cards use compact responsive inline padding. A resize observer measures their intrinsic content plus padding and borders, then uses the shared fitting helper to hide whole trailing days that do not fit, including from screen readers. Hidden cards do not take up row space; visible cards grow to share the remaining width. Days never wrap or show partial temperature ranges. Current and daily temperature ranges share `TemperatureRange`: muted low → primary-text high, using the same stroked arrow icon and explicit low/high labels for screen readers. Each daily card shows its day's weather as an icon at the top right, opposite the day label. The backend chooses the icon (clear, partly cloudy, cloudy, fog, rain, snow or storm) from the weather code, and the frontend only draws it, in the accent colour, with the condition in text for screen readers. The icon overhangs the card's padding so it never makes the card taller, it shrinks on narrower widgets and disappears on very narrow or very short ones, and the temperatures keep their size. On weather widgets at least 960px wide, where cards would otherwise be mostly empty on the right, the icon moves out of the corner and sits large beside the day and temperatures, and on tall enough widgets the temperatures grow as well. Just below that width the temperatures also grow a little (26px) to use the spare width. In every layout a card's content is centered, so any width left over is split evenly on both sides, never piled up on the right. Hourly forecasts keep their single temperatures and four-column presentation. The forecast switch occupies the header's right-hand actions slot with a 44px touch target, rather than floating over the weather content. The forecast and its switch appear only when the daily cards fit whole beneath the current conditions. The widget measures the cards' natural height and compares it with the space left, borrowing up to half of its own bottom padding for a small shortfall. If they still do not fit, the forecast and the switch are hidden together, and a card is never shown clipped or squeezed. The hourly view needs less height than the daily one, so the daily cards decide for both. When the data includes an alert, compact Weather replaces the forecast with alert text, and its shortest presentation shows the warning alone. Weather spacing and responsive rules live in its component.

Smaller widgets show deliberately reduced content rather than scaled content. Compact tiles keep core information: event and time, or bill and amount with due date. Secondary detail gives way before core content clips.
